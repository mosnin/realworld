# Viral Mission Game System & Segment Launch Strategy

## The growth thesis

Realworld grows when a useful Mission makes a specific person feel needed—not when it rewards them for sending links. The viral unit is a **shareable Mission Challenge**: a bounded, legible piece of real work with an outcome, a role that needs filling, a safe first contribution, and a visible record of what changed.

The system has one coherent rule across companies, classrooms, content teams, friend groups, and the open internet:

> Every invitation, unlock, ritual, and recognition must help a group create, verify, or reuse a real Artifact.

No points, streaks, public productivity leaderboards, arbitrary badges, loot, referral rewards, or engagement quotas. Realworld earns a repeat visit by making the world more intelligible and the participant more capable after they act.

## The Mission Challenge

A Mission Challenge is a public, unlisted, or private invitation surface generated from a real Mission state. It is not a generic “join my workspace” link.

### Required challenge fields

| Field | Why it exists |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | Names the concrete thing the group is trying to make or decide. |
| Moment | Explains why help matters now: a Call, Fracture, review window, Branch comparison, or Surge. |
| Role card | Shows one bounded role: researcher, reviewer, builder, tester, storyteller, facilitator, or domain-specific contributor. |
| First Move | A safe contribution that can be understood and started in minutes. |
| Scope and permissions | States access, time expectation, whether work is visible publicly, and what can be changed. |
| Proof target | Defines what a useful contribution looks like and how attribution will appear. |
| Social context | Shows the Mission’s current Pulse and a compact, privacy-safe sense of who is already involved. |
| Exit path | Lets a guest contribute once, follow, or decline without a dark pattern. |

A Mission owner or authorized steward creates a Challenge only from a real durable object. A generic invite is allowed for private teams but is intentionally less prominent because it does not offer a clear contribution path.

### The share card

Every Challenge has a small, shareable visual card that carries a live-safe snapshot: Mission title, outcome, one room/location, a requested role, current moment, and a single action such as **Answer Call**, **Review Branch**, **Join Surge**, or **Remix Proof**. It must never expose private Artifacts, participant names, hidden room activity, or agent detail outside the recipient’s permission scope.

The card is a doorway into a particular work moment. It is not a promotional landing page and it does not turn the sender’s contacts into an address book.

## The conversion loop: guest to durable collaborator

```text
Proof / Call / Challenge
        ↓
Guest sees outcome + bounded role + safe first Move
        ↓
Contributes, reviews, remixes, or joins a Surge
        ↓
Contribution is attributed and changes the Mission
        ↓
Guest chooses: remain scoped contributor, become member, follow, or leave
        ↓
New Proof / useful Call creates the next Challenge
```

### Conversion rules

1. **Guest first, account later when safe.** A public or unlisted Challenge can reveal enough context to evaluate the work before sign-up. Account creation is required immediately before a durable contribution, never before basic understanding.
2. **The least power necessary.** Challenge acceptance grants a bounded contributor/observer role, room scope, and expiry. It never silently confers team-wide access.
3. **One useful first action.** The landing state has one primary Move, not an overwhelming task board or a chatbot prompt.
4. **Attribution before expansion.** After the contribution is accepted, the participant sees what changed, who depended on it, and their visible lineage. Only then do we offer continued membership or another Mission.
5. **Leave cleanly.** A guest can decline, unfollow, or let access expire without social penalty. No notification nags for unaccepted Challenges.

## Role selection as cooperative positioning

Role selection should feel like picking a meaningful station in a co-op system, not completing a personality quiz. The system proposes roles from the Mission’s actual Calls and the person’s explicit interests, demonstrated contributions, availability, and privacy settings. It labels the reason for every suggestion.

| Role mode | What the user sees | Output improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Explore** | Map, Mission brief, public Proof, safe observer view | Lowers context cost before joining |
| **Scout** | Research/verification Call with evidence template | Finds missing information and reduces false confidence |
| **Builder** | Defined Artifact/Move and collaborators | Converts intent into a versioned output |
| **Reviewer** | Proof criteria, diff, and evidence | Improves quality before merge/publish |
| **Connector** | A capability-specific invitation or dependency | Brings a needed contributor, not generic reach |
| **Steward** | Constitution, prioritization, approvals | Maintains coherence and safety |

Role selection is reversible. A participant can complete one bounded Move as a Scout and later become a Builder without losing attribution. The product never scores people for taking a role; its only durable signal is accepted, evidenced contribution.

## Room unlocks: capability, not reward

Rooms are revealed when a Mission’s work creates a legitimate need for that work mode. They are not locked behind engagement or achievement.

| Trigger | Room becomes useful | Why it improves output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission outcome accepted | Mission Core + Workshop | Establishes shared context and first Artifact |
| Source/evidence need appears | Research Observatory | Makes research inspectable and reusable |
| A second valid approach is proposed | Branch Lab | Prevents disagreement from becoming hidden parallel work |
| A proposal meets review criteria | Review Deck | Separates critique from production and creates Proof |
| A specific capability is missing | Signal Tower | Turns a vague need into a bounded Call |
| A time-bounded collective push has a real goal | Surge Hall | Creates coordinated focus and a visible before/after |
| An Artifact becomes reusable | Artifact Library | Makes prior output discoverable and remixable |

An owner can add a room deliberately, but the UI asks for its purpose and first active object. Empty rooms are not shown as "unlocks." A user sees only rooms relevant to their current permission and task context.

## Co-op Surges

A Surge is a voluntary, time-boxed collaborative event with one observable output. It is the core “this feels alive” ritual, but never a meeting replacement or attendance game.

### Surge structure

1. **Declare:** an organizer specifies a target, 25–90 minute window, required roles, permission boundary, and expected Proof.
2. **Assemble:** invitees see exactly why they were called, what they can do, and whether an agent is preparing material. They may join as active, observer, or not now.
3. **Focus:** the Field narrows to the relevant rooms and live handoffs. The steward highlights the next shared constraint; agents offer bounded assistance only.
4. **Resolve:** the group produces an Artifact, a verified decision, a merge, or a clear Fracture with a recovery owner.
5. **Close:** a brief records before/after, contributors, evidence, and the next asynchronous Move. The Surge does not create a streak or attendance rank.

### Surge social texture

- Shared live presence, role handoffs, and visible progress are the fun; reactions are limited to contextual acknowledgements (for example, “I can review,” “blocked,” “ready”) that advance coordination.
- The timer is a planning aid, not a threat. Late joiners receive a concise catch-up brief; early leavers remain credited for accepted work.
- The outcome transformation appears on the Mission World only when a real Proof or durable state changes.
- Background sound is optional and off by default. No ambient pressure, pop-ups, or forced voice.

## Meaningful progression and reputation

Progression has two layers: **Mission capability** and **portable contribution record**. Neither is a score chasing engagement.

### Mission capability

Mission capability grows as its team creates reusable, verified structures: clear Constitution, reliable Artifacts, evidence practices, healthy review, tested playbooks, and successful handoffs. This unlocks more complex collaboration patterns—for example, connected Missions, protected Branch merges, sponsored Calls, or public remixing—only when the required trust and governance evidence exists.

### Portable contribution record

A participant or agent accumulates a transparent, contextual record of:

- Artifacts authored, reviewed, remixed, or materially improved;
- Proof accepted, including criteria and evidence;
- Calls answered and handoffs completed;
- demonstrated capabilities, bounded by scope and expiry where appropriate;
- peer acknowledgements that are tied to a specific accepted contribution.

The record supports better role matching. It must not become a universal social score, secret rank, hiring signal, or automatic grant of authority. Participants can hide public profile elements, contest attribution, and remove themselves from public discovery subject to Artifact/license history.

## Proof, remix, and fork loops

Proof is the growth object. A completed, permissioned Proof can become a public discovery surface, a reusable Artifact, or a remix seed.

### Remix/fork contract

1. A creator marks a Proof/Artifact as private, internal, unlisted-shareable, or public-remixable with a license/attribution policy.
2. A viewer sees the outcome, evidence, lineage, constraints, and the smallest useful way to adapt it.
3. Selecting **Remix** creates a new Mission or Branch with a durable parent reference, copied only within permitted scope, and a new Constitution/outcome prompt.
4. The remix retains attribution to source contributors, visibly distinguishes what was copied from what changed, and can propose a merge-back or connected-Mission relationship where policy allows.
5. A new Proof from the remix becomes a fresh Challenge for a different audience.

The loop rewards reuse of useful work, not cloning. A remix that adds no differentiated output has no new Proof or reputation gain.

## Seasonal cross-Mission events

Seasonal events create temporary public coordination around genuine opportunities: a civic data sprint, a campus showcase, an open-source hardening week, a creator collaboration festival, or a themed research challenge. They are opt-in, time-bounded, and connected to a useful output category.

| Event component | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Shared prompt | Broad enough for varied Missions; concrete enough to yield compatible Proofs |
| Contribution protocol | Defines reusable formats, license/privacy choices, verification and moderation rules |
| Cross-Mission Calls | Match needs between Missions without exposing private content |
| Showcase | Surfaces verified Proof and lineage, not volume of activity |
| Closing artifact | Produces a shared index, toolkit, dataset, or learning record |
| Safety | Opt-in visibility, clear age/organization policies, abuse moderation, no public ranking |

A seasonal event should leave the ecosystem more useful after it ends. If it cannot create a reusable result or relationship, it is not an event Realworld should run.

## Spectator-to-contributor flow

Public discovery begins with a Proof, a challenge card, or a connected-Mission map—not an infinite feed.

1. **See:** spectator opens a permission-safe Proof or Mission Challenge and understands the outcome, current moment, and provenance.
2. **Orient:** a compact map tells them what they may see and where work is happening; private rooms/people remain absent rather than blurred curiosities.
3. **Choose:** they can follow quietly, save a reusable Artifact, answer a public Call, review a Branch, or remix allowed output.
4. **Contribute:** the first Move is bounded, role-scoped, and has an explicit review path.
5. **Belong or depart:** after attribution, they can become a member, retain scoped contributor access, follow future Proof, or leave.

Discovery quality is defined by the fraction of spectators who make a useful contribution or intentionally save/follow a relevant Mission—not total session length, impressions, or scrolling.

## Team formation

When a Mission needs more than one person, the Steward generates a **formation brief**: goal, missing roles, expected contribution size, working window, room scope, decision owner, privacy level, and a first Proof target. The brief can become a Challenge or be sent directly.

Formation supports three modes:

- **Known circle:** owner chooses people directly; members receive a purpose-scoped invitation and can propose roles.
- **Capability match:** Signal Tower recommends opted-in contributors with relevant, transparent evidence. The Mission owner chooses whom to invite; no automatic assignment.
- **Open Call:** publicly discoverable bounded contribution with moderation and eligibility policy.

The system stops forming a team when its real role needs are met. It never floods a Mission with participants merely because public attention is available.

## Segment-first experience flows

The product system is consistent; the first Mission should vary by permission, pacing, and source of the Challenge.

### 1. Solo creator

1. Creator names an outcome, boundary, and first Proof target.
2. Steward proposes Mission Core + Workshop, three Moves, and one review/verification question.
3. Creator makes a real Artifact or approves a bounded agent draft.
4. On first visible progress, the system offers one specific Challenge: ask a reviewer, researcher, or collaborator for a complementary contribution.
5. Creator chooses private, unlisted, or public visibility before sharing.

**Success:** useful Artifact and share-worthy, permissioned Challenge within 20 minutes; no invite prompt before a real need exists.

### 2. Invited collaborator

1. Invite opens directly to outcome, inviter context, requested role, access scope, and first Move.
2. Collaborator can inspect the relevant Artifact/Proof context before accepting.
3. Accepting grants the least required role and enters the relevant Room, not a general dashboard.
4. They complete or decline the bounded Move; a reviewer/owner verifies the result.
5. They see durable attribution and choose whether to remain, follow, or leave after scope expiry.

**Success:** a contributor understands the need in under two minutes and contributes without a meeting.

### 3. Student / classroom

1. Teacher creates a private class Mission template with age/organization policy, roster/import choice, learning outcome, team size, review rubric, and public-sharing default set to private.
2. Student joins through teacher-controlled invitation or approved roster identity; selects a project role from legitimate team needs.
3. Teams work in scoped Rooms; the Steward offers process scaffolding but cannot publish, contact outsiders, or access restricted student data.
4. Review Deck uses a transparent rubric and contribution lineage; teacher controls Proof visibility and export.
5. Optional showcase produces opt-in, de-identified or approved public Proof only after required consent.

**Success:** every student can find a legitimate role and produce reviewable work; no public profile, open discovery, direct messaging from strangers, or competitive ranking by default.

### 4. Company team

1. Admin/owner creates a private organization Mission with SSO/identity policy, data classification, retention, allowed providers/tools, approval controls, and external-sharing disabled by default.
2. Team enters through approved org identity and receives Mission/room scope from existing roles.
3. Steward proposes work and bounded agent runs only within company policy; every action is traceable and approval-gated where necessary.
4. A company Surge resolves a concrete decision or ships an Artifact, then records Proof/replay for internal reuse.
5. A reusable internal template can seed future private Missions; external remix/Call requires an explicit owner policy transition.

**Success:** faster visible coordination and reusable knowledge without data leakage, shadow membership, or uncontrolled agent activity.

### 5. Content crew

1. Producer starts a Mission from a release, story, campaign, or series outcome and sets rights/credit policy.
2. Mission World separates research, production, review, and publication readiness into purposeful Rooms.
3. A Challenge calls a collaborator for a concrete contribution—fact check, edit, visual treatment, audience research—not a vague “collab.”
4. A shared Surge closes a cut/script/package; Review Deck creates Proof with credits, sources, and publish approval.
5. Permissioned Proof becomes a behind-the-scenes artifact, remixable template, or audience Call where rights permit.

**Success:** contributors see credit/approval boundaries upfront and the crew produces a publishable, attributable package.

### 6. Casual friend group

1. A friend starts a lightweight private Mission: plan a trip, produce a zine, organize a community night, build a game mod, or solve a shared problem.
2. The product asks for one desired outcome and picks simple Core + Workshop; no enterprise setup or role hierarchy is required.
3. Friends choose small voluntary Moves and can start a short Surge when they want to decide or make something together.
4. A completed Artifact/Proof creates a natural memory and optional reusable template, not a score.
5. The group may share a specific Call with another friend only when a need emerges.

**Success:** the group reaches a shared result faster than a chat thread and leaves a useful artifact behind.

### 7. Open internet challenge

1. Organizer publishes a moderated, policy-bound Challenge with clear eligibility, license, contributor role, review path, privacy/visibility, and dispute/report route.
2. Spectator sees a public Proof/context brief, chooses a bounded Call or remix route, and creates an account only before durable contribution.
3. Contributor works in a restricted/public-safe room; automated and human moderation defend against spam, unsafe content, impersonation, and prompt injection.
4. Reviewers verify contributions before public Proof/reputation applies; suspicious activity is quarantined rather than amplified.
5. Accepted Proof connects back to contributors and source Mission, creating next-generation challenges.

**Success:** strangers make valuable, attributable contributions without organizers sacrificing safety or quality.

## Safety, anti-spam, and segment privacy

### Universal safeguards

- Calls and Challenges require a real Mission object, role, scope, and first Move; generic blast invites are rate-limited and de-emphasized.
- Public contribution has progressive trust: read → scoped draft → reviewed contribution → broader permission; reputation never auto-grants authority.
- All public Claims/Proof require provenance, clear author/agent attribution, review status, and report/appeal path.
- Rate-limit invitations, follows, open Calls, remixes, agent launches, reactions, and profile discovery; detect repetitive low-value remixing and coordinated spam.
- Keep Challenge audience controls explicit: private, named invitees, organization, unlisted link, eligible community, or public.
- Do not use contact scraping, notification nagging, autoplay share prompts, engagement rankings, or behavioral advertising.

### Classroom safeguards

- Private-by-default class spaces; teacher/guardian/organization policy determines identity, discovery, export, and sharing.
- Age-appropriate defaults, roster-limited membership, teacher-controlled external invites, and no public reputation/profile discovery for minors.
- Clear student contribution/AI-use record, accommodations for role/pacing, and accessible asynchronous equivalents for Surge.

### Company safeguards

- Organization-bound identity and policy, data classification and retention controls, approved model/provider routes, audit/export, and owner-controlled external sharing.
- No cross-company discovery, remix, or AI context transfer without an explicit policy/consent boundary.
- Admins can disable public Calls, seasonal events, profile discovery, and presence detail.

## Measurement and quality gates

Metrics are diagnostic, privacy-respecting, and segment-specific. They must not be shown as public rankings or used to pressure individuals.

### Definitions

| Metric | Definition | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Activated Mission** | Mission produces an Artifact, accepted Move, or reviewable Proof within 24 hours | Empty room creation does not count |
| **Activated invitee** | Invitee completes an accepted bounded contribution within seven days | Joining/following alone does not count |
| **Useful Challenge conversion** | Qualified challenge recipients who make an accepted contribution | Count only permissioned, non-spam distribution |
| **Contribution K-factor** | Average number of *new activated contributors* attributable to one activated contributor’s Challenges/Proof/remix lineage over a cohort window | Excludes self-invites, duplicate accounts, coerced/paid referrals, and mere sign-ups |
| **Proof reuse rate** | Public/internal-eligible Proofs that are forked, remixed, or referenced and produce a distinct downstream Artifact | Copy-only forks do not count |
| **Return-to-work retention** | Activated users who return to a Mission and perform/view a meaningful next action in D7/D30 | Do not optimize for passive opens |
| **Safety quality** | Rate of confirmed abuse, privacy violations, spam, and false/contested Proof per eligible action | A growth win cannot offset safety regression |

### Initial quality gates (to be calibrated by segment pilots)

| Area | Initial gate | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo activation | ≥60% of moderated new solo users create a real Artifact or accepted Move in 20 minutes | Session recording, durable readback |
| Invitee activation | ≥40% of accepted purpose-scoped invitees complete a reviewed first contribution in seven days | Cohort event analysis + sample review |
| Challenge relevance | ≥70% of surveyed Challenge recipients say they understood why they were invited before account creation | Moderated testing / in-product research |
| Contribution K-factor | Demonstrate `K ≥ 0.20` for an activated cohort before expanding public distribution; growth decision requires quality and safety gates too | Fraud-filtered cohort analysis |
| D7 return-to-work | ≥35% of activated users return for a meaningful Mission action | Durable action cohort analysis |
| D30 return-to-work | ≥20% of activated users return for a meaningful Mission action | Durable action cohort analysis |
| Proof reuse | ≥15% of eligible Proofs generate a distinct verified downstream reference/remix within 30 days | Lineage analysis + quality sample |
| Surge quality | ≥70% of moderated Surge participants report the session advanced a concrete output; ≥80% can name its resulting Artifact/decision | Survey + durable Proof check |
| Spam resistance | <1% of eligible public Challenge recipients report spam/irrelevance; confirmed abuse stays below launch threshold set with Trust team | Reports, moderator adjudication, rate-limit telemetry |
| Privacy | Zero critical classroom/company boundary violations in pilot; all reported issues have audit/recovery evidence | Role tests, security review, incident records |

K-factor is a release signal, not a design target in isolation. A cohort with high sharing but low accepted contribution, weak retention, user-reported pressure, or safety failures is a failed loop.

## Segment launch sequence

Launch in contained cohorts, prove output quality, then widen the social surface.

1. **Founders and content crews (private/unlisted):** fastest feedback on Artifact, credit, review, Surge, and Challenge clarity.
2. **Small company design/engineering/product teams (private):** validate policy, audit, agent boundaries, reusable internal Proof, and 2–15 person real-time coordination.
3. **Teacher-led classrooms or cohorts (private, consented):** validate role clarity, scaffolding, accessible collaboration, and stringent minors/privacy controls before any public school marketing.
4. **Curated open challenges (moderated, invite/community gated):** validate spectator-to-contributor and anti-spam behavior with strong human review.
5. **Public remix and seasonal events:** only after contribution quality, moderation, identity/reputation, privacy, and operational recovery gates are proven.

Each segment has a specific exit condition; Realworld does not claim network effects from a waitlist, spectator traffic, or unverified user growth.

## Top product decisions to close

1. **Initial beachhead:** this specification recommends starting with small content/product crews, where real artifacts, roles, fast cycles, and shareable Proof are naturally present. Classroom and open-internet challenge modes should remain policy-gated until privacy/moderation readiness is independently proven.
2. **Public identity default:** public Proof should credit contributors only through explicit opt-in identity/display settings; pseudonymous public contribution must be supported from the first public pilot.
3. **Challenge eligibility:** public Calls should begin with curated/approved Missions and progressive trust, not open publishing for every new account.
4. **Seasonal event ownership:** events need a named moderation/operator owner and a reusable contribution protocol before the product promotes them; they cannot be automatic engagement campaigns.
